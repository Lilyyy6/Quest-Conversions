How do I Quest Convert?

Step 1: Finish the PC version

This will make things easier.

Step 2: Prefab

Make sure all the stuff you added to the PC avatar is saved to a prefab (this makes updating the avatar alot easier). If you don't have a prefab, click and drag the avatar from the scene into Assets, and it will make a prefab.

Step 3: Quest Version

Drag the PC prefab into the scene, making a duplicate of the PC version. If the new avatar in the scene is different from the existing PC avatar, you did something wrong - go back to the prefab step.

Step 4: Materials

Click on the first mesh and click on the material in element 0. This will show you where that material is in Assets.

Click on the material in Assets and click Ctrl + D to duplicate (this only works in unity, Ctrl + D in most other programs deletes, so be careful). Then change the duplicated material shader to the correct quest compatible shader. Here is a guide on what shader to use for each material:

if it is

-opaque

-shiny/matcap

-metallic/specular

-emissive/glowing

-has more than one texture layer (detail, decal, etc)

-audio link

Then use VRChat > Mobile > Toon Standard

if it is

-transparent/translucent

-cutout

Then use VRChat > Mobile > Particles > Additive or Multiply

Additive keeps light values, while Multiply keeps dark values. You need to pick which one to use based on the texture or color of the material. Also, the particle shaders get no light data or any layers or anything, so only use them for simple things such as glass, fishnets, etc.

“My mesh is cutout but its the main piece/I don't want it to be see-through”. Sacrifice the cutout for the opaque Toon Standard. You can edit the texture and color the cutout bits to match the texture, so that its less noticeable.

  • Quest

The particle shaders literally only get a texture slot - thats it. The tiling doesn't even work.

For Toon Standard, you want to look at the PC material and see what it does. Does it have specular/reflections? Matcap? Detail Layers>? Hue Shift? Audio Link? You can take the textures and masks from the PC material and put them in the matching slots on the Quest material.

  • PC

  • Quest

For shading, you can use flat, or whichever setting matches the PC material most closely. If your PC avatar uses lilToon, then you can right-click the PC material and click lilToon > Generate Ramp. This will generate a shadow ramp that you can use for Custom shading in Toon Standard. Set the Shadow Tint to 0 to make it appear properly.

Limitations:

Toon Standard can only have one extra texture as a layer, known as the Detail layer. It also only has one Matcap slot and one emission slot, with the emission mask and color texture being combined into one texture slot. Audio link only has one effect per material for Quest. Some changes might have to be made to the textures or some features might need to be discarded when making the Quest material match the PC one as close as possible.

  • PC

  • Quest

I dropped the less important Matcap for the shiny metal one.

You can combine the main texture and any extra detail/decal layers in a texture editor. The detail layer in Toon Standard should be used for a layer that needs its offset controlled by an animation (scrolling, etc), or needs to be tiled (you can tile any image in an image tiler in your browser, but its a little bit more annoying).

Once you finish your first duplicated material, click and drag the new material into element 0 on the Quest version. Make sure everything looks right.

Do this for every material on every mesh :). Some meshes will re-use materials, so you can just slot in the exisitng Quest material you made instead of making a new one.

Step 5: physbones

Quest avatars only get 8 physbone components and 64 physbone transforms. A component is literally the component you see in the inspector with all the settings. A transform is every bone the component effects or controls. There are tips to reducing physbone components and transforms in the Physbone Section
